# Class stock-prediction experiment

The experiment ran from August 18, 2026 to November 12, 2026. Each portfolio started with $100,000, and no rebalancing occurred during the experiment.

Portfolio 1 was selected according to students' interests. The classroom code describes Portfolio 2 as indicator-based, but the exact indicator was not documented in the available files.

Across 45 paired teams, the mean final value was $101,179 for Portfolio 1 and $103,087 for Portfolio 2.

Portfolio 2 exceeded Portfolio 1 for 30 of 45 teams. Its estimated mean advantage was $1,908.89. The 95% confidence interval was -$379.72 to $4,197.50, with a two-sided p-value of 0.0999.

The personal five-stock portfolio ended at $118,390. MRNA generated most of the positive gain, while NVDA and NVO lost value. This concentration shows how a strong portfolio outcome can depend heavily on one unusually successful stock.

## Interpretation

Portfolio 2 had a higher average outcome, but its dispersion was also larger, and the uncertainty interval for its mean advantage included zero. A short-period winner may reflect chance, company-specific news, or a favorable market environment rather than a persistent predictive relationship.

Stock prediction is difficult because prices respond quickly to new information, relationships change across market regimes, and flexible models can fit historical noise. Reliable evidence therefore requires evaluation on unseen dates and comparison with simple benchmarks.
